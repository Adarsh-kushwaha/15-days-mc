How the rounds actually run

Each round is a gate. The interviewer's mind is usually made up before the next one starts.

round 01

Opening act

What is JavaScript, var vs let, hoisting, closures, IIFE, == vs ===, ES6. Concise, or rejected.

round 02

Can't ignore

Output-based questions, the small programs, simple polyfills. Never complex — the interviewer has to be able to do it too.

round 03

Discussion

Promises and every variation, async/await, callbacks, event loop, this, prototypes, debounce, throttle, delegation.

round 04

Eliminator

Storage trade-offs, DOM APIs, performance, security loopholes, custom events, vanilla DOM builds.

  • 01Your interviewer is an employee who left their own work to be here, and may have taken ten interviews already today.
  • 02You can answer 20% correctly and get selected. You can answer everything and still get rejected. Connection is the variable.
  • 03Treat every question as a conversation, not a test. Think out loud — silence during a machine coding round reads as nothing happening.
  • 04Admit unknowns fast: "I haven't worked on this, I'll look into it after the call." Then reason from first principles.
  • 05Some questions are domain-specific and unpreparable. Expect one. Even the best batsmen get out.
